## Context

Pitch these as **exclusive data stories**, not product announcements. Tech journalists aren't interested in "we launched a tool." They're interested in "here is data nobody else has."

The hook: Pulse has the only public, live, verified dataset of 2026 Nigerian tech salaries. The findings are newsworthy.

## Pitch A — TechCabal

**To:** Editor / Reporter covering Nigerian tech workforce  
**Subject:** Exclusive data: Nigerian tech salaries gained 3% since 2023. The naira lost 70%.

---

Hi [Name],

I'm reaching out with data I think TechCabal's readers would find valuable — and that nobody has reported yet.

Pulse is an anonymous Nigerian tech salary index I launched this week. 135 records. 2023 community data combined with live 2026 submissions. The methodology is public and the limitations are stated clearly.

**The headline finding:** The median monthly gross for Nigerian tech professionals moved from ₦581,000 in 2023 to ₦600,000 in 2026 — a 3% nominal increase. In the same period, the naira went from ₦450/$ to ₦1,580/$. The median salary in dollar terms went from ~$1,290/month to ~$380/month.

That's not stagnation. That's a ~70% real-wage collapse measured in any hard currency.

Other data points worth exploring:
- Professionals who negotiate earn ~13% more than those who don't — and under half negotiate
- The senior-to-junior salary multiplier is 2-3×
- Fintech dominates submissions; healthtech and edtech comp data is almost entirely absent
- A small number of diaspora/remote workers in the same dataset earn 8-12× their onshore peers

I can offer TechCabal exclusive first-look access to the full dataset for a data story, an interview, or a first-person piece from me on the methodology and findings. Happy to provide underlying data, methodology documentation, and quotes.

The story isn't "person builds salary tool." It's "here's the first public evidence of what devaluation has done to Nigerian tech compensation — in workers' own words and numbers."

Interested?
