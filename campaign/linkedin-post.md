**VERSION A — Data-forward (recommended for first post)**

I've spent the last few months building something I wish had existed when I was negotiating my first tech role.

It's called Pulse — an anonymous salary index for Nigerian tech professionals. Real submissions. Real numbers. Built on 135 compensation records and growing.

Here's the headline finding:

The median Nigerian tech salary moved from ₦581,000/month in 2023 to ₦600,000/month in 2026. That's +3% nominally.

In the same period, the naira went from ₦450/$ to ₦1,580/$. The same salary is now worth less than a third of what it was in dollar terms.

If your salary hasn't moved in three years, you have taken a real pay cut. The number on your payslip just didn't tell you.

**Other things the data shows:**

→ Professionals who negotiated their last salary earn ~13% more than those who didn't
→ Senior (4-8yr) median is roughly 2.5× the junior median — experience is the biggest single lever
→ Fintech dominates the dataset, but healthtech, edtech, and SaaS are catching up
→ Benefits packages vary wildly — same gross salary, very different total compensation

All of it is live at the link below. You can explore by function, level, and industry. You can drop in your salary and see where you land in the distribution. Everything is anonymous — no name, no email, no company asked.

The index improves every time someone contributes. If you're in **Data & Analytics**, **Product Design**, **outside Lagos**, or in a **non-fintech sector** — those are the segments we need most right now.

Four minutes. No signup.

**VERSION B — Story-first (for follow-up post or reshare)**

A few weeks ago, someone DM'd me asking what a "good" salary offer looked like for a Senior PM role in Lagos.

I didn't know what to tell them beyond my own experience and a few conversations with friends.

That's the problem. Nigerian tech salary data exists — it's just locked in DMs, spreadsheets shared between trusted friends, and whispered conversations in office corridors.

The information asymmetry is a real cost. It falls hardest on people who are earlier in their careers, newer to a company, or don't have a strong network in exactly the right place.

Pulse is one person's attempt to fix a small part of that problem. 135 anonymous records from real tech professionals. Clean data, honest methodology, public and free.

It will be a lot more useful with more records. If you've ever benefited from a salary conversation with a colleague or friend, consider this the same thing — just at scale.
